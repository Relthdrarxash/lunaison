For health professionals

Lunaison is a free, open source (GPL-3.0) Android menstrual cycle tracker designed so patients can log their cycles, symptoms and prepare for medical appointments with no account, no subscription and without any health data ever leaving their phone.

Read the medical evidence dossier

Status. Lunaison is a self-observation and health education tool, not a medical device or a method of contraception. It never makes a diagnosis and encourages patients to seek medical advice whenever an unusual pattern is logged.

Why recommend Lunaison to patients?

  • Zero intimate data collection: the app does not even request the Android INTERNET permission. Unlike commercial trackers funded by ads or subscriptions, no data (period dates, pregnancy plans, symptoms) can ever be sent to a third-party server.
  • A consultation summary readable in 30 seconds: from Understand → My cycle report, the patient can display or share a one-page summary (median length and regularity of the last 6 cycles, bleeding duration, moderate/severe pain days, intermenstrual spotting, recurring symptoms by phase).
  • Suited to diverse life stages: Naturally irregular cycles or perimenopause mode (PCOS, menopausal transition, with optional tracking of hot flashes, night sweats and pelvic pain), Pause predictions mode (pregnancy, postpartum, continuous contraception), and optional ovulation signs (cervical mucus and basal body temperature).

Clinical reference ranges and referral cues

Regularity thresholds and non-alarming prompts to consult a clinician are based on international guidelines (FIGO 2018, ACOG Committee Opinion No. 651, NICE NG88, NHS, HAS / CNGOF):

  • Cycle length: adult reference range of 24 to 38 days (cycle-to-cycle variation up to 7 to 9 days depending on age); adolescent reference range of 21 to 45 days.
  • Period duration: gentle prompt beyond 8 consecutive days of bleeding.
  • Heavy menstrual bleeding: practical NHS indicators (soaking through protection every 1 to 2 hours for several hours, clots larger than 2.5 cm).
  • Dysmenorrhea and pelvic pain: calm prompt when severe pain interferes with daily activities or recurs across cycles, supporting timely evaluation for secondary causes (endometriosis, adenomyosis, fibroids).
  • Extended delay: 5 or more days past the estimated window, a neutral card outlines common causes (delayed ovulation, stress, pregnancy, normal cycle variation) without alarmist messaging.
