How it works

No magic, just your cycles

Lunaison does not guess anything: it reads what you have logged, applies a few simple, public rules, and always shows you the uncertainty. Here they are.

The four stages of a cycle

A cycle starts on the first day of a period. The lengths below are an example: for most people, it is the first half that varies, while the second stays fairly stable at around 12 to 13 days.

How Lunaison estimates your next period

  1. Recognizing periods. A day with real bleeding starts a period; spotting alone does not. Up to two days without an entry in the middle do not split a period in two.
  2. Measuring your cycles. Each cycle runs from one first day of a period to the next. A gap of less than 15 days or more than 90 days is probably a missed entry: it is left out of the calculation.
  3. Taking the median of your last six cycles. The median is the middle value: an unusual cycle (stress, illness, travel) does not throw it off, unlike an average.
  4. Giving a range. Its width depends on how much your cycles vary: from 2 days either side if your cycles are very regular, up to 7 if they vary a lot. Without a history, it starts from what you entered at the beginning, with a 4-day margin.
  5. Estimating the phases. Ovulation is placed about 13 days before the next period, because this part of the cycle varies little. It is an estimate, not a measurement.

The "worth mentioning" alerts

When your notes move away from medical reference ranges, Lunaison points it out with a link to the relevant guide. It is never a diagnosis: just something to mention to a doctor, a gynecologist or a midwife.

Where your data goes

Nowhere. Lunaison does not have permission to access the Internet: even by mistake, it could not send anything. The only way out is you.

Auditability: exact formulas and source code files

Lunaison embeds no artificial intelligence and no black-box model: every calculation relies on purely deterministic rules grouped in the core/cycle module with no Android dependency, and verified by automated unit tests on every build.

2. Plausible cycle filtering & median Only completed cycles lasting 15 to 90 days (MIN_PLAUSIBLE_CYCLE, MAX_PLAUSIBLE_CYCLE) are kept, over the last 6 cycles (CYCLES_FOR_PREDICTION = 6). CycleAnalyzerTest.kt
3. Forecast range width (uncertainty) With 3 or more completed cycles: half-width spread = clamp(ceil(σ), 2, 7) days around the median date, where σ is the standard deviation of your last 6 cycles. With 2 cycles: ±3 days; without history: ±4 days (PROVISIONAL_SPREAD). CycleAnalyzer.kt
4. Ovulation and cycle phases Estimated ovulation at next_period - 13 days (LUTEAL_DAYS = 13), with a ±2-day ovulation window (OVULATION_WINDOW = 2). Optional fertile window: from ovulation - 5 d to ovulation + 1 d. CycleAnalyzer.kt

Symptom / phase correlation A symptom or mood is linked to a phase only if it appears in at least 2 cycles (MIN_CYCLES_FOR_STATS = 2), in at least 50% of tracked cycles, and at least 2.0 times more frequently inside that phase than outside (PATTERN_ENRICHMENT = 2.0, once ≥ 5 days outside the phase are logged).
