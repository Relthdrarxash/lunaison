What the studies say

Every tip has its source

Here, in plain language, are the studies Lunaison relies on. For each one, a badge says whether the topic is well studied, promising but to be confirmed, or still backed by little evidence. Full references are at the bottom of the page.

  • Well established consensus or many studies
  • To be confirmed encouraging results, limited studies
  • Little evidence a lead, nothing more

The "normal" cycle is far more varied than people think

One study analyzed more than 600,000 cycles logged in an app. The average cycle lasted 29 days, but only two thirds fell between 25 and 30 days.

Well established

Reference ranges for an adult cycle

A cycle of 24 to 38 days, periods of 8 days at most, less than 7 to 9 days' difference between the shortest and longest cycle.

FIGO consensus, 2018

Well established

In the teenage years

In the first years, cycles are often irregular: 90% last between 21 and 45 days. Three months without a period warrants a medical opinion.

ACOG, 2015

Well established

Ovulation does not fall on day 14

It is the first part of the cycle that varies; the second lasts about 12 to 13 days. Hence Lunaison's estimate, counted backwards from the next period.

Bull et al., 2019

What relieves period pain

Well established

Anti-inflammatories (ibuprofen…)

The most studied treatment, more effective than paracetamol (acetaminophen). Best taken as soon as the pain starts.

Marjoribanks et al., Cochrane, 2015

To be confirmed

Heat

Hot water bottle or heat patch: an effect close to that of anti-inflammatories in the available trials, which are small.

Jo and Lee, Scientific Reports, 2018

To be confirmed

Regular physical activity

Around 45 to 60 minutes, three times a week, it may clearly reduce pain.

Armour et al., Cochrane, 2019

Little evidence

Ginger

A few small, encouraging trials of low quality.

Daily et al., Pain Medicine, 2015

Well established

Pain that stops you from living is not normal

It can point to endometriosis, which affects about one in ten people who menstruate and takes 7 to 12 years on average to be diagnosed in France.

ACOG, 2018; Kvaskoff (Inserm), 2024

Food and premenstrual syndrome

Well established

Iron

Before menopause, the reference intake is 16 mg a day. Meat, fish, lentils, chickpeas, tofu; vitamin C in the same meal helps absorb it. No supplements without a blood test.

EFSA, 2015

To be confirmed

Calcium for premenstrual syndrome

A randomized trial showed fewer symptoms with a daily calcium intake.

Thys-Jacobs et al., 1998

Little evidence

Omega-3

A modest effect on pain in a meta-analysis. Oily fish, walnuts and canola oil provide them.

Mohammadi et al., 2022

Little evidence

Eating or training "for your phase"

Nothing solid justifies a diet by phase. For sport, differences in performance across the cycle are tiny and uncertain: how you feel remains the best guide.

McNulty et al., Sports Medicine, 2020

Period products and contraception

Well established

Tampons and cups

Clean hands, the wearing time in the leaflet respected, the lowest possible absorbency. Sudden fever, rash, feeling faint: remove the product and call emergency services.

Anses, 2025

Well established

An app is not contraception

Even the most rigorous cycle-awareness methods often fail in typical use: up to about one person in four within a year.

Trussell, Contraception, 2011
